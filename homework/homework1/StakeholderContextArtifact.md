# Stakeholder Memo -- Unemployment and Stock Market Returns
**Stakeholder:** Portfolio Manager 
## Problem
Does the U.S. unemployment rate provide useful information about subsequent stock market returns?
## Why This Matters
Employment conditions influence the amount of disposable income individuals have to put towards investments. Thus, greater employment may be associated with greater participation and demand in the stock market, while greater unemployment may have the opposite effect. If unemployment rates contain information about subsequent stock market performance, then it could be useful as a factor when evaluating future market conditions.
## Proposed Analysis
This project aims to compare the U.S. unemployment rates with subsequent stock market returns following unemployment reports. Unemployment rates will come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, and stock market data will come from yfinance. The primary metric will be the correlation between unemployment rates and subsequent market returns over a defined time period.
## Decision/Use
The purpose is to determine whether unemployment rates provides useful, predictative information that could be considered alongside other factors when evaluating future market conditions.
## Known Limitations
Stock market performance is influenced by many different political, economic, and financial factors. Since these factors may occur simultaneously, the analysis may not be able to isolate the effects of unemployment on stock market returns.
